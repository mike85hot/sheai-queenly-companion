# UNICEF FemTech application brief

Source: [official UNICEF call](https://www.unicef.org/innovation/FemTechCall), reviewed 23 October 2026.

## Opportunity

- Deadline: **4 November 2026**.
- Selected companies can receive up to **US$100,000**, 12 months of technical mentorship and access to UNICEF's network.
- The call seeks companies registered in a UNICEF programme country, working with a functional prototype and committed to open-source licensing and principles.
- Women-led companies and founders under 35 are strongly encouraged. Do not infer that the team must be exclusively women or that this is a mandatory age cutoff from the wording on this page.
- Safety-by-design, data protection, consent and contextual validation are central to the call.

## SheAI's positioning

The closest match is **Pillar 3: Mental wellbeing, safety and protection**, which includes gender-based violence, safe reporting, information and referrals. Position SheAI as browser-based safety support reducing barriers to help, with AI-assisted information and everyday well-being. Be clear that the AI does not currently detect violence or predict risk and that the platform is not a clinical mental-health intervention.

Evidence available in the current source includes a public SOS experience, country-confirmed emergency directory, simulated Safe Exit Call, account-based journey sharing, trusted contacts, AI assistant modes, a rule-based pre-trip journey check and an OpenStreetMap-based nearby-lodging finder with a hotel check-in checklist. Automated SMS dispatch, offline PWA support, AI-based or crime-data risk prediction, clinical validation and documented population-level outcomes are not established.

## Required confirmations and evidence

- Confirm the applicant's legal company status and registration in a country accepted by the call's linked programme-country list; users in Nigeria do not prove Nigerian incorporation.
- Review the actual application form and linked eligibility guidance before asserting qualification. The headline page is not a complete eligibility assessment.
- Publish a reviewed public source repository with MIT LICENSE, reusable source and clear dependencies; confirm its URL works while signed out. Hosted AI models and managed services remain separate from the code license.
- Establish private security reporting, privacy/consent and retention/deletion procedures; verify safeguards before enrolling adolescent users.
- Provide a truthful live demo and test evidence. The current unit tests are not an independent security audit, clinical study or fresh self-hosting validation.
- Propose local co-design and measurable outcomes without presenting prospective campus pilots or partnerships as completed.
